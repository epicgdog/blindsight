# Insight pitch script (target under 3:00 at 150 words per minute)

> Stage notes and headings are not counted. Say only measured or sourced numbers: counts from the scan itself, and the public statistics listed with sources in NOTES-part2.md. Slide numbers refer to slides/blindsight.pptx. Slides 8 (What works today) and 12 (Built for our tracks) are for questions and track judges; click past them in the timed pitch.

## 0:00 to 0:25 Problem (slides 1 to 4)
Insight lets blind and low-vision people tour a property independently, guided by voice, using CoStar 3D data.

43 million people are blind worldwide, and more than 7 million Americans have vision loss that glasses cannot fix. If you cannot see, you cannot browse listings. GPS stops at the front door, and camera apps describe a scene without knowing the building. So someone has to come with you.

## 0:25 to 0:50 Insight (slide 5)
But CoStar already holds a detailed 3D record of millions of spaces. Matterport had scanned more than 14 million when CoStar bought it. This building, Rabinowitz Hall, has 66 scan points, each a 360 degree photo at a known position. Nobody has turned that into guidance.

## 0:50 to 2:00 Live demo (slide 6)
[Volunteer is blindfolded at the first taught place, holding the phone upright in portrait. One person talks, one drives the laptop, one guides the volunteer.]

Our volunteer cannot see. The phone streams its camera to our laptop. Listen.

[Location cue plays, for example: "View matches the front door."]

The laptop compared the live view with every scan of the building and confirmed the match with geometry. On screen you can see which scan it chose and how sure it is.

[Volunteer turns toward people or a bench. Cue: "2 people at 1 o'clock and a bench at 11 o'clock."]

That is object detection, spoken as clock directions.

[A teammate walks toward the volunteer. Cue: "Person directly ahead, getting closer."]

[Only if the voice agent is running: volunteer holds the talk button and asks "How do I get to the entrance?" The phone answers.]

A person who cannot see just learned where they are and what is around them, in real time.

## 2:00 to 2:35 How it works, use cases and what is next (slides 7 to 11)
Phone video goes to the laptop. We detect objects, match the frame against the building's scans with geometric verification, and speak short cues back. Hold to talk, and a Gemini voice agent answers from tools that know where you are and what the camera sees.

The same pipeline works in any scanned space: a home tour, an office listing, a campus, a hotel or a hospital.

Next, we test with blind users and on real phones, then add room to room routes and Vexcel imagery from the street to the door. That part is roadmap, not built.

## 2:35 to 3:00 Why it wins (slide 13)
For people with vision loss, this means walking into a home, an office or a clinic on their own. For CoStar, it is a new audience on Homes.com, Apartments.com and LoopNet, and a feature competitors cannot copy without this data.

Insight lets blind and low-vision people tour a property independently, guided by voice, using CoStar 3D data.

This is just the first step to a more visible future.
